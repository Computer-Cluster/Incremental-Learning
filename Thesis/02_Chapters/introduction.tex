\section{Introduction}

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a field of knowledge that seeks to develop machines capable of emulating human behaviour and reasoning, enabling interaction almost indistinguishable from that with another human being. This area not only focuses on the creation of intelligent systems but also on developing tools that facilitate and optimise everyday activities.\\

A subfield of AI is Machine Learning, which studies algorithms capable of autonomously learning tasks. Within this field, Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) are among the most recognised techniques, as they employ mathematical processes to solve complex problems. ANNs have proven useful in areas such as predicting 5G network capacities based on daily traffic \cite{zhao2022}, as well as in the classification of materials such as metals and rocks using fuzzy logic \cite{salazar2013}.\\

ANNs are composed of artificial neurons that simulate biological ones. In this context, the chemical processes that occur in the brain are computationally replicated through signals that travel between artificial neurons, hereafter referred to as ``neurons''. This distributed and parallel structure grants ANNs a remarkable learning capability \cite{liu2015}.\\

In the context of machine learning, when a technique such as ANNs focuses on learning a specific task, it is considered a memoryless linear algorithm. Since its origins, this has been one of the most widely used approaches in ANNs \cite{GiraudCarrier2000}. However, when it becomes necessary to incorporate new information without retraining the entire model, the concept of Incremental Learning arises, which seeks to include new data without having to train the model from scratch.\\

A related aspect derived from incremental learning is the concept of memory in AI, which explores how a machine learning algorithm can forget previously acquired information when incorporating new data. This problem, analogous to human memory loss, poses challenges for both machines and human beings. In response, several approaches have been designed to mitigate this phenomenon. One example is the work of \cite{bullinaria2009}, which proposes the use of ANNs with dual weights, where the first layer of weights behaves as short-term memory and the second as long-term memory. The experiments conducted in \cite{bullinaria2009} demonstrate improvements in incremental learning tasks, reducing information loss compared with previous implementations such as the Learn++ algorithm \cite{li2008, Elwell2011}.\\

This research aims to explore new configurations of duplicated weights, expanding and extending the results obtained in \cite{bullinaria2009}.\\
