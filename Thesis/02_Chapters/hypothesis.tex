\section{Hypothesis and Evaluation Criteria}

\subsection{Research Hypothesis}

Incremental learning in artificial neural networks faces the challenge of balancing the stability of previously acquired knowledge with the plasticity required to incorporate new information. Within this context, the following research hypothesis is proposed:

\begin{quote}
    \textbf{General Hypothesis:} 
    The incorporation of additional duplicated memories in a neural architecture based on the model of \cite{bullinaria2009} will increase the retention of previously acquired knowledge by at least 10--15\% compared with the original model, while maintaining a comparable computational cost and without requiring explicit replay mechanisms.
\end{quote}

This hypothesis is based on the premise that the duplication of synaptic weights acts as a distributed consolidation mechanism, analogous to the transfer processes from short- to long-term memory observed in biological systems.  
Thus, the aim is to demonstrate that the inclusion of multiple intermediate memories can mitigate catastrophic forgetting and promote a smoother transition between incremental learning phases.

\subsection{Evaluation Criteria} \label{sec:criterios_eval}

To validate the proposed hypothesis, the following quantitative and qualitative evaluation criteria are considered, applied to each incremental block of the experiment:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Average Incremental Accuracy (IA):} a measure of the overall performance of the model, considering the progressive accumulation of knowledge throughout the training blocks.
    
    \item \textbf{Forgetting Rate (F):} the percentage loss of accuracy on previous datasets after learning a new block, calculated as:
    \[
    F = \frac{A_{i-1} - A_{i}}{A_{i-1}} \times 100
    \]
    where $A_{i}$ represents the accuracy obtained on the previous dataset after training block $i$.
    
    \item \textbf{Memory Retention (RM):} the proportion of knowledge preserved at the end of incremental training, comparing the final performance with the initial one:
    \[
    RM = \frac{A_{f}}{A_{0}} \times 100
    \]
    where $A_{f}$ is the final accuracy and $A_{0}$ the initial accuracy.
    
    \item \textbf{Computational Cost (CC):} the average training time per block and resource usage, aiming to determine the efficiency of the proposed architecture compared with the baseline model.
    
    \item \textbf{Stability–Plasticity (EP):} qualitative analysis of the balance between the preservation of prior knowledge and the model’s adaptability to new tasks.
\end{itemize}

The results obtained for each metric will be analysed comparatively among the model variants (with different numbers of duplicated memories) and against the reference scheme proposed by \cite{bullinaria2009}.  
The objective is to determine whether the proposed approach improves knowledge retention without compromising adaptability or significantly increasing computational cost.
